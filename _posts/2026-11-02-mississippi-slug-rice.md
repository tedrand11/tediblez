Scroll through food feeds today and you’ll see the internet run wild with the name "Mississippi Slug Burger."

While old-school culinary history remembers the original Mississippi slugburger as a Depression-era nickel patty stretched with soy flour, social media creators have reimagined the term into something far punchier: smash patties laced with the legendary "Mississippi Pot Roast" trinity—ranch seasoning, au jus gravy mix, and tangy pepperoncinis with a splash of brine. On TikTok and Instagram, that holy trifecta turns an ordinary griddled burger into a hyper-savory, salty-sour flavor bomb dripping in caramelized onion and pepper juice.

Mississippi Slug Rice takes that exact viral burger flavor profile off the griddle and folds it straight into a one-pan bayou skillet.

### Instructions
### Detailed Step-by-Step Instructions

#### 1. Sweat the Aromatics & Brown the Ground Beef
Set a large, deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium heat with a splash of oil or butter. Toss in your diced yellow onion and allow it to cook down slowly until soft, fragrant, and slightly translucent (about 5 to 7 minutes). 

Meanwhile, heat a separate pan over medium-high heat and brown the 1 lb of ground beef, using a wooden spoon to break it up into bite-sized crumbles. Once browned through with some caramelized edges, remove from the heat and thoroughly drain off the excess grease so the rice doesn't end up oily. Set the drained beef aside.

#### 2. Toast the Rice in Seasonings & Brine
Turn the heat under your onion skillet to medium. Dump in the 1 cup of dry, uncooked rice directly onto the softened onions. Immediately sprinkle the packet of au jus seasoning and the packet of ranch seasoning over the grains. 

Pour in the reserved pepperoncini juice from the jar (about 1/4 cup). Stir continuously for 2 to 3 minutes. You want the raw rice grains to drink up the brine, coat themselves in the seasoning dust, and lightly toast. This step develops deep toasted flavor and helps keep the grains firm and distinct during the simmer.

#### 3. Pour Broth & Return Beef
Slowly stream in the 1 3/4 cups of beef broth, using your spoon to scrape up any flavorful brown bits stuck to the bottom of the pan. Stir the liquid until the seasoning packets dissolve completely into a savory gravy base. Add the browned, drained beef back into the skillet and gently stir until meat, rice, and broth are evenly distributed.

#### 4. Top Layer & Simmer
Scatter your 10 to 15 diced pepperoncinis evenly over the surface of the rice mixture—**do not stir them in yet**. Leaving them resting on top keeps their sharp, tangy bite bright while the rice simmers below. 

Bring the liquid up to a gentle simmer, clamp on a tight-fitting lid, and drop the heat down to low. Let it cook undisturbed for 18 to 20 minutes until the rice has fully absorbed the broth.

#### 5. Rest, Fold & Finish
Turn off the heat and leave the lid on for an extra 5 minutes to let the steam settle and finish plumping the rice. Remove the lid and use a fork or flat spatula to gently fold the diced pepperoncinis throughout the grains. 

Finish by dashing Tabasco across the top to ur likin’ for that authentic bayou kick, and serve it piping hot straight from the skillet.

---

### Cook's Notes

* **The Pepperoncini Juice:** Pouring the brine directly into the dry rice infuses the grains with acid and salt before the broth is added.
* **Toasting the Grains:** Browning the dry rice in the seasoned base keeps the grains firm and flavorful as they simmer.
